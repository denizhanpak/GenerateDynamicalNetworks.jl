Synchrony

Status: stable with the default seed, experimental with `seed=:race`

With the default seed it passes the acceptance protocol on most of the benchmark cases; with the race seed it fails half of them. See Benchmark results below.

Phase oscillators in pairs. Each pair is either in phase or in anti-phase, and a state of the graph is one pattern of in-phase and anti-phase pairs. The network of states lives in synchrony space: every oscillator keeps the same frequency throughout, and what changes is which pairs are synchronised.

model = fit(spec, Synchrony())              # the default seed
model = fit(spec, Synchrony(; seed=:race))  # the race seed

The system

$2M$ oscillators with one natural frequency, in $M$ pairs. In the frame rotating at that frequency, each oscillator of pair $\sigma$ obeys

\[d\theta_{\sigma,k} = \big(a_\sigma \sin\Delta_k + b_\sigma \sin 2\Delta_k\big)\,dt + \varepsilon\,dW , \qquad \Delta_k = \theta_{\sigma,\text{other}} - \theta_{\sigma,k} ,\]

where $a_\sigma$ and $b_\sigma$ depend on the other pairs through the cosines of their phase differences. The coupling therefore involves more than two oscillators at a time, in the spirit of Bick (2018) and Bick and Lohse (2019). The phase difference $\psi_\sigma$ of a pair then obeys

\[\dot\psi_\sigma = \sin\psi_\sigma\,\big(g_\sigma + k_\sigma\cos\psi_\sigma\big), \qquad g_\sigma = -2a_\sigma,\quad k_\sigma = -4b_\sigma .\]

Two things follow from the factor $\sin\psi_\sigma$.

  • In phase (S, $\psi_\sigma = 0$) and anti-phase (D, $\psi_\sigma = \pi$) are invariant for every pair, whatever the other pairs do.
  • The $2^M$ patterns of S and D are equilibria. They are the corners of an $M$-dimensional cube, and moving along a cube edge flips one pair.

At a corner, the rate at which pair $\sigma$ moves away from its current value is $\lambda_\sigma = k_\sigma + g_\sigma$ if it is in phase and $k_\sigma - g_\sigma$ if it is in anti-phase. Because $g_\sigma$ and $k_\sigma$ can be chosen separately for every pattern of the other pairs, every rate at every corner is free. A cube edge that is left at a positive rate and arrived at with a negative one carries a connection from one corner to the other.

A frame is read from the signs of $\cos\psi_\sigma$, which give the corner.

The design

The layout

sync_layout places the graph on the cube.

  • Each state is a corner.
  • Each edge is a path of single flips from its state's corner to the next state's. Corners along the way are hidden transit corners, and time spent there counts towards the state being left.
  • Edges out of one state start along different pairs, and no corner is used by two paths. A two-way edge needs two separate paths.

The search tries random placements, for the fewest pairs that work and then the fewest transit corners.

using GenerateDynamicalNetworks
const GDN = GenerateDynamicalNetworks

spec = example("foraging"; noise=0.1)
layout = GDN.sync_layout(spec.P)
layout.M, [GDN.corner_name(v, layout.M) for v in layout.corner_of], GDN.transits(layout)
(3, ["DDS", "SDS", "DDD", "SSS", "DSS"], 2)

Five states on a cube of three pairs (six oscillators), with two transit corners.

The rates

corner_rates fills in every rate from one departure rate per edge.

WhereRate
leaving a state along an edgethat edge's departure rate, which the fit adjusts
leaving a transit cornera fast rate
every other direction at a used cornercontracting, at the same fast rate
unused cornerspointing back towards the network

The fast rate is four times the largest rule-of-thumb departure rate.

The starting rates

With the default seed=:rough, each departure rate starts from a rule of thumb: the time to leave a saddle at noise $\varepsilon$ is about $\ln(1/\varepsilon)/\lambda$, so

\[\lambda_{ij} = \frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\tau_i}\,\sqrt{P_{ij}\cdot(\text{number of successors of } i)} .\]

The fit

correct_rates corrects the departure rates from simulation.

  • Each pass simulates the model on new seeds: 32 runs of 250 times the sum of the dwells. The last three passes use 96 runs at half the step and take smaller steps.
  • For each state, the pass compares the measured dwell and branch ratios with their targets and takes one Broyden step on the departure rates of the state's edges. How the measurements respond to the rates is learned from pass to pass.

A departure rate may be corrected to below zero. The corner is then weakly stable, and noise has to push the pair over a small barrier: that state has moved to the excitable side. This happens for long dwells at high noise, where even a rate of zero leaves the corner too soon.

OptionDefaultMeaning
seed:roughthe starting rates: :rough or :race

A fit can be run in batches with checkpoint and deadline, as for the CTRNN design.

The race seed

Status: experimental

With seed=:race the backend fails half of the benchmark cases.

The rule of thumb treats each exit on its own. seed=:race starts instead from a calculation of the whole race out of a state. Each exit is close to a one-dimensional diffusion of one phase difference, whose waiting time distribution can be computed on a grid; a state's exits race each other, which gives the dwell and the branch shares that any set of rates should produce (race). The seed solves for the rates that give the target dwell and shares, and the correction passes start from there, with their first response taken from the same calculation.

In the benchmark suite the race seed passes fewer cases than the default (Results). All of its failures are a single branch check on one set of runs, each within about a percentage point.

In the research prototype the race seed made the difference on one graph that isn't in the suite: one with branches of about 1% out of slow states, which failed with the default seed and nearly passed with the race seed (research/notebooks/phase6_synchrony.jl).

Limits

  • Size. The layout search stops at seven pairs (14 oscillators) and throws an ArgumentError if no layout is found. The cube doubles with every pair.
  • Branch errors. Every failure in the benchmark suite is a branch check; every dwell is inside its band. With the default seed, two of the four failing cases miss on two checks in the same direction.
  • Rare branches from slow states are where the default seed struggled in the prototype, as above.
  • Only mean dwells are fitted.
  • Two-way edges are laid out, but the backend was run on the one-way example networks only.

Most of the models are classified as heteroclinic. The rings with long dwells at noise 0.1 come out as unknown. On the three-state ring with dwells 1, 3 and 10, the fitted departure rate of the longest state is slightly negative and the other two are positive, so one corner is on the excitable side and two are saddles. The classifier's noise-free runs then end at the stable corner from some starting points and at a saddle from others, and starting points that disagree give the class unknown.
